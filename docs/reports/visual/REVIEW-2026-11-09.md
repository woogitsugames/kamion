Scale measurements below were taken from the 1 m grid in ortho views (A2: ~151 px/m side, ~190 px/m front) and from known dims in perspective views.

### Render-pipeline vs asset issues — the key distinction
Everything in A2/A5/world-c is offline-rendered (Cycles path tracing or EEVEE). The target is URP on a Mali-G57-class GPU (Samsung A16): no real-time GI, no SSR on Medium, 2–4 cascade shadows at modest resolution, baked AO + light/reflection probes, ASTC textures, MSAA instead of 512 spp. Therefore:
- **Asset issues** (shape, proportion, material values, missing objects) are real now and will stay real in Unity. They are the priority.
- **Pipeline issues** are tagged `[PIPELINE]`. Several things that look acceptable in Cycles (A5 interior fill light, soft contact shadows, glass) will look *worse* in URP. Nothing here proves the look survives on the phone; checklist item 2 fails everywhere.

### Per image

**`kierowca_overcast.jpg` / `kierowca_lowsun.jpg`** (most important frame in the game)
- Red flags hit: near-uniform black plastic; hood as a separate smooth shell; background reads as cardboard.
- Defects (ranked):
  1. **P0 — Steering rim bisects both round gauges.** From the eye point, the rim's upper arc passes over the left air-pressure gauge (x≈400–520 px) and the right fuel gauge (x≈930–1045 px), and clips the outer ring of the on-screen speedo. The README says "górny łuk nie zasłania zegarów"; that holds for the central screen only. Air pressure is safety-critical, and real EU cabs (Actros MP5, FH5, S-Way, XG) put the whole cluster inside the upper rim window. Fix: narrow the cluster to fit the rim window seen from `eye` (about 300–330 mm wide at that distance), or drop the physical round gauges (owner Q2) and integrate both air circuits and fuel into the 12.3" screen. Add an automated sightline test: rim torus vs. the cluster *bounding rectangle*, not only the hood.
  2. **P0 [PIPELINE] — Value structure.** Dash top, hood, rim, pillar and curtains all sit at albedo 0.03–0.04 and render as one black mass. In Cycles the bounce light from the bright windscreen lifts them a little. In URP Medium (probes plus baked AO, no bounce) this frame will crush to black against a clipped-white windscreen: the classic "phone sim cab" look. Fix: (a) check the hood and dash-top albedo against measured soft-touch (≈0.04–0.06 linear is fine for the hood, but the lower dash and pillar trim should step up clearly); (b) **render the same camera now with EEVEE (no GI, a single probe) or in a URP scene** to see the real target before adding more detail.
  3. **P1 — Hood (kaptur) shape.** It is a single smooth, uniformly bevelled shell with no parting line to the dash, no texture break, no speaker or ambient-light slot. It still reads "stuck on top" (the art-bible red flag about the TV on the dash), less so than iteration 1. Real hoods blend into the dash-top skin with a visible seam and a matte grain finish. Fix: merge the hood into the dash-top surface with a split line about 3 mm wide, and use a grain normal with the same scale as the dash top.
  4. **P1 — The cluster shows speed twice** (big "82" plus an analog speedo on the same screen, plus 2 physical dials): 5 instruments in 0.45 m. Real 2020s clusters show one primary speed readout. Clean it up together with fix 1.
  5. **P2 — Background.** The forest is a flat sawtooth silhouette, the hall is an untextured white box, the lamp mast has no head, and the parking grid is perfect. It is render-only, but it is two-thirds of the frame and biases every judgement of the interior ("this looks fake"). Fix: use a CC0 HDRI with a backplate (Poly Haven, e.g. an overcast parking or industrial set) for the control renders; record the licence in LICENSES.md.
  6. **P2 — lowsun:** soft vertical "haze wedge" at x≈740–800 (the shadow edge of the cab blurred into fog). It looks like volumetric haze, which is not wanted. Also, at 9° sun the dash top gets no veiling reflection in the windscreen. A real cab shows a faint dash reflection here; it is a strong realism cue. Decide whether the glass shader should show it (URP: reflection probe plus fresnel on the windscreen at low intensity).
- Good, keep: eye point and windscreen bottom line (the view of the road ahead is right for a cab-over); the black frit band; the cluster content (AMT "A 12 E", CC/LIM, 2-circuit bar, AdBlue, odometer, time, °C), typographically calm in Inter; glass reflection on the cluster.

**`zegary_overcast.jpg`**
- P1: the left round gauge disappears behind the screen bezel. It reads as a dial *behind* the display, not beside it (geometry intersection or depth). Give each dial its own bezel ring flush with the screen housing.
- P1: the dash face under the cluster is a perfectly flat dark plane with no grain visible at 0.6 m. The ART_BIBLE requires grain normals; they are either too weak or the tile scale is wrong. Verify the soft-touch normal strength (it should be visible as a sheen break at grazing angles).
- Good: the font, the red needles, the 1/min ×100 legend, the green economy arc, the 10.4/10.2 bar values (plausible service pressures).

**`pasazer_overcast.jpg` / `pasazer_lowsun.jpg`**
- P1: **The passenger dash is a vast featureless slab** (x 900–1600). Real cabs have a storage tray with a rubber mat, a document or tablet holder, a speaker or defrost grille, a split line to the glovebox lid, and often a cup holder. Right now it is the single biggest "CG" area of the cabin.
- P1: **Steering wheel spokes are round tubes** (3 cylindrical spokes). That is a 1990s bus or tractor look. Modern EU truck wheels have broad flat spokes carrying the switch pods, and the pods are part of the spoke, not stuck-on blocks.
- P1: **lowsun:** the dash top lights up to an almost beige-white patch. With albedo 0.042 that means a strong sheen or specular at grazing incidence. Check the roughness and sheen on soft-touch. Real soft-touch stays dark-grey and slightly velvety, not light.
- P2: the door card is flat with primitive handle shapes; add a door-trim parting line and the armrest-to-panel radius.
- P2: the main mirror's back housing is white (the placeholder cab colour). Swap it for a dark plastic mirror shell when A3 lands.
- Good: the centre-console angle toward the driver, the AMT selector placement, the inboard armrest, the curtains on the rail.

**`kierownica_overcast.jpg`**
- P1: the hub airbag cover reads as an inflated pillow with a soft, slightly melting silhouette (a docs/06 red flag: "rozmyte, topiące się bryły"). Real covers are a firm moulding with a crisp tear seam. Tighten the hub shape and keep the bevel radius at 2–4 mm.
- P1: the switch pad is an add-on block with oversized keycap gaps (~4 mm). It looks like a toy keypad. Real pods have flush keys with 0.5–1 mm gaps and a shared bezel.
- P2: the rim is smooth with no stitching seam on the inner side and no thumb contours at 9/3 o'clock.
- Good: the reflection of the rim on the cluster glass, the screen legibility at an angle, the button glyphs.

**`fotel_overcast.jpg`**
- P1: the leather is uniformly smooth: no cushion creases, no compression wrinkles at the bight, no shine on contact areas. That reads as new vinyl. Add a crease normal layer driven by curvature and contact (seat front edge, lumbar).
- P1: the shape is a flat block: the cushion has a square edge, there is no crowning of the bolsters, and the backrest and bolster meet at a sharp 90°. Real truck seats have pronounced cushion crown and bolster roll.
- Good: perforation only in the centre panels, double topstitch, pebble grain on the bolsters, rails and lever placement.

**`lezanka_overcast.jpg`**
- P1: **"fotel jak klocek" (an explicit ART_BIBLE red flag)** — from behind, both seats are rounded slabs with no visible headrest, no belt webbing, no rear storage pocket shape, and no mechanism. This is how the player sees the seats when walking to the bunk.
- P2: the console switch matrix (12 rockers) reads well. The navigation screen is tiny relative to the 10.1" spec. Check the scale (it looks about 7").
- Good: the asymmetric roller blinds (one lowered), the overhead lockers, the window framing.

**`wariant_trim_pasazer.jpg`, `wariant_trim_fotel.jpg`, `wariant_znak_piasta.jpg`**
- The trims read correctly and stay desaturated (no "gaming" accent). Koniak and piasek give the interior the mid-tones it is missing. As a critic I recommend **piasek or koniak** for the visual reasons in defect 2 above, but the choice is the owner's.
- Hub badges: the TRAKT and NAREW marks are clean. The hub silhouette shows polygon facets on its lower-right curve. Increase the segments or add a normal-smoothed bevel.

**`ao_check_kierowca.jpg`, `ao_check_lezanka.jpg`, `ao_atlas_preview.jpg`**
- P1: the AO is noisy (speckle on the dash top and under the hood), which means too few bake samples or no denoise. Low-frequency mottling turns into visible blotches in URP. Bake with ≥ 256 samples plus a denoiser, or blur the AO.
- P1: the AO contrast is weak overall: the footwell and under-seat areas are not dark. In URP this AO is the main contact shadow, because Cycles' bounce light will be absent.
- P1: the atlas is mostly black: hidden or internal faces, plus hundreds of tiny islands. That wastes texels. Remove hidden faces, and give texel priority to surfaces the driver sees (dash top, hood, rim, door card, A-pillar).
- Known (README): glass and curtains are baked in. Exclude them.

## 2. A2 — truck exterior concepts (owner: `truck-artist`)

**Verdict: PASS as a blockout for the owner's direction choice; FAIL as a design language versus the "modern EU long-haul" brief.** Scores: scale/proportion 8 · silhouette and character 4 · EU realism 5 · readability of the renders 8.

### Checklist §5
1 PASS (fixed cams, 1.80 m figure, 1 m grid) · 2 n.a. (concept) · 3 FAIL (no reference sheet beside the concepts) · 4 PASS (this) · 5 PASS for dimensions (auto-measured; width ≤ 2.546 m, length 6.07–6.13 m) · 6 see the verdict.

### Measured from the ortho views (A_bok, C_przod)
Height 3.92–3.95 m · tyre Ø ≈ 1.06 m (315/80 R22.5 ≈ 1.08 m) · wheelbase 3.70 m · front overhang ≈ 1.44 m · cab length ≈ 2.38 m · cab width 2.49 m · windscreen bottom/top ≈ 2.07/3.03 m · bumper clearance ≈ 0.36 m. **All are plausible for the class.**

### Defects common to A/B/C
1. **P1 — The cab floats over the front wheel.** The side views show the cab floor at ≈1.14 m sitting on a separate square lower-front block, with the wheel exposed under a thin fender line. Real cab-overs have a wheelhouse moulded into the lower cab corner and step panel, with a deep mudguard (top section on the cab, lower section on the frame). This is the single biggest "matchbox truck" cue (an ART_BIBLE red flag).
2. **P1 — Box corners.** A and C have near-sharp front vertical corners. Every modern EU cab has large front corner radii or wrap-around corner deflectors (R ≈ 0.25–0.5 m in plan) that reach back to the door. B is the only one that approaches this.
3. **P2 — The wheels are solid black discs** with a flat hub and no tread or sidewall break. Fine for clay, but they dominate the "toy" read. Even a blockout benefits from a tyre/rim split and a tread band.
4. **P2 — The steps** cover only ≈0.35 m behind the arch, against a ≈1.15 m door. Verify against the photo (★ "okolica koła przedniego ze stopniami").

### Per concept
- **A · TRAKT (`koncepcja-A_*.jpg`)** — a horizontal-slat grille plus small rectangular twin lamps reads as a 1990s European design (DAF 95 / EuroStar era), not 2020s. The visor in body colour adds to the dated look. Good: an honest long-haul box, a clear sleeper volume. Risk: generic.
- **B · BIELIK (`koncepcja-B_*.jpg`)** — the closest to a modern EU silhouette (19° rake, rounded roof, light band, side extenders). Problems: the trapezoid grille and two slim lamps are generic and too small for a 2.5 m face; the front looks blank between the windscreen and the bumper. **No class V/VI (kerb/front) mirror** is visible on B's front and side views. With camera mirrors, EU trucks still need class V/VI coverage, usually by a physical mirror or extra cameras; show it. The deflectors are fine. The roof-edge marker lamps are good.
- **C · NAREW (`koncepcja-C_*.jpg`)** — vertical bars plus round twin lamps in a steel bumper read as a US vocational truck crossed with a 1980s Jelcz. It is a strong character, but it contradicts "nowoczesny europejski ciągnik long-haul" (`docs/07` §1). The visor with amber markers and the grab rails are good functional details worth keeping in whichever concept wins.
- **`porownanie-koncepcji.jpg`** — a clear sheet. The paint choices (steel blue, bottle green, ochre) follow the ART_BIBLE palette.
- **`logotypy.png`** — clean vector marks. The wordmarks use a generic rounded monoline. Acceptable for now, but the brand will need a custom wordmark later. The TRAKT mark is very literal. The NAREW mark holds up best at small size.

**Recommendation (non-binding, the owner decides):** B's cab volume and glazing, plus C's functional details (visor markers, grab rails, exposed chassis cues), plus a new, larger, distinctive front (headlamp graphic as the brand signature). Fix defects 1–2 at A3 before any detailing.

## 3. World tile C v1 (owner: `world-builder`)

**Verdict: FAIL as a visual (reads as a 2014 mobile game); PASS as road-geometry pipeline output.** Scores: geometry/topology 8 · scale cues 5 · materials 3 · vegetation 2 · Polish realism 3 · lighting 5 (EEVEE) · artefacts 5.

### Checklist §5
1 PARTIAL (driver eye height is present; no "skrzyżowanie z wysokości oczu" in town context, no rain/night) · 2 FAIL (Blender only) · 3 PARTIAL (ortho only; no eye-level photo) · 4 PASS (this) · 5 PARTIAL (RMS vs NMT reported; no albedo histogram, no object-under/over-terrain check reported) · 6 FAIL.

### Per image

**`driver_andersa_to_putry.jpg`**
1. **P0 — Trees are low-poly "lollipops".** Faceted icosphere crowns on tall straight sticks, the same height, evenly spaced, with pinkish trunks. This is exactly the docs/06 §2 flag ("powtarzające się drzewa", mobile-game look). The real stands near Białystok are Scots pine with orange-brown upper bark and grey lower bark, irregular flat-topped crowns, and birch and alder at the forest edges with understorey. Fix: proper pine/birch assets (cards for the crown, tapered trunk, 3–5 species/size variants, random lean and scale, edge shrubs), impostors beyond 150 m.
2. **P0 — Everything off the carriageway is lawn.** Uniform mid-green, no mown/unmown split, no dry grass, no bare soil at the kerb line, no sidewalks or bike paths (the ortho clearly shows red kostka paths along Andersa).
3. **P1 — Kerbs.** Bright, clean, continuous extrusions with a tall reveal (looks >15 cm) and no 1 m segment joints. Polish urban kerbs are grey concrete 15×30 segments (or granite) with visible joints, dirt lines, and often a kostka gutter strip (ściek) or opaska. Reduce the albedo to ~0.25–0.3, add joints, dirt and a gutter.
4. **P1 — Asphalt** is a single noise texture. It needs aggregate scale, longitudinal seams, patches, crack sealing, wheel-path polish and ruts (docs/06 §4 specifies all of these).
5. **P2 — The left median strip has a bluish tint** (a kerb or bridge material leaking). Check the material assignment.
6. Known/accepted: no lamps, signs, barriers or acoustic screens. But note: the ortho shows **acoustic screens on Andersa**, and they will dominate the real view. Plan them for v2, not "later".
- Good: lane widths, median, arrow markings, the slip-road kerb radius, the vertical alignment. The road reads correct in plan.

**`putry_above.jpg`**
- P0: industrial plots (Kwadrat Park) are lawn up to the walls. In reality they are concrete or asphalt yards, parking lots, fences and gates (see the ortho). The buildings are untextured boxes floating in grass.
- P1: the viaduct deck lacks a cornice, visible thickness and piers. The ring passing under the deck shows almost no shadow or occlusion band, which flattens depth (verify the sun direction; under a ≈30 m-wide deck there must be a dark band).
- Good: kostka truck apron on the roundabout island; lane arrows; railing rhythm.

**`kwadrat_park_gate.jpg`**
- P0: a "gate" with no gate: no fence, no barrier, no gatehouse, no paved apron. A 7 m access road with kerbs runs across a meadow between boxes. This is one of the frames a truck driver sees most (depot entrance).
- P1: the Z-shaped kerb notch is still visible (acknowledged), and the kerb runs as a raised white band along an industrial road where flush edging or none is typical.
- Fix: model yards from BDOT10k/OSM `landuse=industrial` and `amenity=parking`, plus fences and gates as authored overrides for the base and depot entrances.

**`dk8_dk19_exit.jpg`**
1. **P0 — Wrong cross-section for the road class.** DK8 at the forest exit is a dual carriageway without kerbs: a paved or gravel shoulder, a grass verge, and steel safety barriers (median and embankments). The render shows urban kerbs along a 100 km/h road. A Polish driver spots this immediately.
2. **P0 — The bridge has a railing only, painted bright white like a garden fence.** Polish road bridges carry a steel safety barrier plus a galvanised grey railing on a concrete cornice. White reads toy-like.
3. P1: the trees (as above) cast blocky shadows on the road that read as dark decals. The crown silhouettes are too opaque and too regular.
4. P1: a faceted terrain crease on the right berm (y≈540–600) and a lump inside the left median (y≈640–700). Smooth the verge slopes and enforce a flat median profile.
- Good: the alignment and curvature, the edge-line widths, and the forest density relative to the ortho.

**`biezenstwa_above.jpg`**
- P1: zebras (P-10) end in grass on several arms. Pedestrian crossings without sidewalks are an instant "generated" tell. Either generate the sidewalks and paths from OSM, or suppress the crossings where no footway exists.
- P1: grass-to-asphalt boundaries are jagged ("torn paper" edges) at x≈1250–1600, y≈600–650 and along several islands. Clip the terrain to the kerb or edge polygon.
- Good: the deck now passes over the ring correctly (fix 2 in the README is confirmed), and the island kostka ring reads well.

**`ortho_putry_overlay.jpg`**
- A very good QA artefact: the alignment is credible. It also documents what is missing: red-roofed houses, sidewalks, parking lots, mixed deciduous trees, acoustic screens. Use it as the v2 checklist.

**`before_r1_*`, `before_r2_*` (×2), `before_r5_*`**
- They confirm the fixes. Scrambled ribbons are gone, the deck/terrain conflict is gone, and the solid parapet was replaced. In `before_r2_dk8_*` the terrain was hilly. Check that the current version keeps realistic relief (Podlasie is gently rolling, not flat, around the DK8 valley).

### `[PIPELINE]` notes for world
EEVEE at 64 spp with AO looks softer than URP will. In URP expect harder cascade shadows, no AO between road and kerb unless baked or decaled, and visible grass tiling (already seen in DrivingLab). Plan the road decals, kerb AO strip and terrain detail texture **in URP**, not in Blender.

## 4. DrivingLab graybox — UI and readability only (owner: `gameplay-ui`; renderer items: `rendering-perf`)

Graybox geometry is intentionally ignored. **UI verdict: FAIL (fixable in a day).**

**`editor-lab-chase-hud.jpg`, `editor-lab-cab-eye.jpg`**
1. **P1 — Layout overlap:** the GAZ panel (top edge y≈455 px) overlaps the bottom of the D and P buttons (y 418–480). Two touch targets overlap, so there will be mis-taps on the phone.
2. **P1 — Translucent controls let the world bleed through:** in the chase view a world sign reads as UI text "−0 m" inside the "−" gear button, and road lines cross the pedal panels. Use opaque or near-opaque (α ≥ 0.85) control backgrounds, or a subtle backing.
3. P1: the "600 obr/min" label is small, grey on slate, and low contrast (estimated < 3:1). The rpm bar has no scale. Raise the contrast to ≥ 4.5:1 and the size to ≥ 14 dp.
4. P1: the cab camera at the current FOV shows no mirror image. The left mirror is a black slab occluding about 13% of the screen. With the A5 80° FOV, neither main mirror is in frame either. Decide on head-look and mirror insets now. A docs/06 §6 rule: the reversing mirror must be usable.
5. P2: the default Unity font. Unify on Inter (already licensed for the cab displays).
6. P2: the on-screen steering wheel (≈290 px, opaque-white rim) is the most salient element on screen. Reduce the opacity, or tint it to the slate palette.
7. P2: the world sign "START — przyspieszanie / hamowanie" overflows its board (the text is wider than the plate).
- Good: a calm slate palette with no neon, glassmorphism or emoji; Polish labels; a clear P/N/D/R state colouring; big pedal targets.

**`editor-lab-overview.jpg`, `editor-lab-surfaces.jpg`, `editor-lab-obstacles.jpg`, `editor-lab-truck-34.jpg`** — `[PIPELINE]` observations for `rendering-perf`
- Grass texture tiling is plainly visible (checkerboard at all distances). Kostka turns into moiré noise at 20–40 m (mip bias or filtering).
- Shadows are stair-stepped (truck, sign poles): the cascade resolution or distance is not tuned.
- The default procedural skybox gives a saturated blue gradient and a hard horizon seam. That is the "Unity default" look. Replace it with a calibrated sky/HDRI and set tonemapping (ADR, docs/06 §4).
- Thin dashed lines break up at distance (no MSAA, or the wrong mips).
- The marker labels on the far signs are unreadable beyond ≈30 m. Fine for a lab, but in the world the signs need proper mip and SDF text.

**`editor-autopilot-r50-telemetry.jpg`** — a dev chart. It is legible on desktop, with small axis fonts. It is not a player-facing asset, so no visual verdict.

- World: data-true road geometry checked against the GUGiK ortho, NMT deck fixes, P-codes on the markings, the kostka roundabout apron, deterministic generation.
- UI: a sober palette and Polish labelling; nothing neon.
